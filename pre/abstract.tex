The design of deep tunnels involves various geotechnical and structural parameters, as well as the need to predict the convergence and stability of the tunnel section. The field of deformations and stresses around the opening is defined by a series of interrelated factors, such as the depth, the geometry of the section, the anisotropy of the stresses \textit{in situ}, the heterogeneity of the rock mass, the construction process and support system adopted, the rheology of the rock mass and the interaction between the elements of the tunnel system. A significant factor in rock masses is the frictional parameter, which refers to the dependence of the resistance on the hydrostatic component of the stresses. Another recurring aspect of this problem is the delayed behavior over time, and one of the biggest challenges is modeling the interaction between the instantaneous and delayed behavior of tunnels. In addition to this complexity, twin tunnels present significant challenges, since the proximity between them can induce significant deformations and also generate an extremely disturbed state of stress, requiring analysis of the massif's non-linear behavior. In addition, the construction process of twin tunnels adds difficulties compared to single tunnels, since the effects of excavating one tunnel on the other must be taken into account. Elasticity and elastoplasticity checks and analyses were carried out, as well as applying the coupled elastoplastic-viscoplastic model. It was possible to verify the influence of the frictional parameter on the behavior of deep twin tunnels, using the Drucker-Prager and Mohr-Coulomb flow functions. The proximity of twin tunnels is an important parameter for understanding the effects that the excavation of one tunnel has on the other. In this context, this study aims to adapt the APDL used in the analysis of deep twin tunnels in order to take into account the offset of the excavation faces, so that it is possible to approximate the reality of the construction of these tunnels, taking into account non-linear and time-dependent constitutive models for the massif and the lining, as well as investigating the effect of the frictional parameter on the convergence profiles and the different stiffnesses for the lining. The aim is also to implement the Mohr-Coulomb surface in the elastoplastic-viscoplastic subroutine developed by \citeonline{Quevedo2017} and \citeonline{Quevedo2021}.
